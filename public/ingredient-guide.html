Skincare labels can feel like a chemistry exam. This guide breaks down the most important ingredients you'll find in your Velmira products — what they do, how to use them, and how to layer them safely. Knowledge is the best skincare tool.

💧 Hydrators & Moisturisers

Hyaluronic Acid

Must-Have

Also listed as: Sodium Hyaluronate

A humectant that can hold up to 1,000× its weight in water. Draws moisture from the environment and deeper skin layers to the surface, plumping and smoothing fine lines instantly.

All skin types Fragrance-free Serum / Moisturiser
Pro tip: Apply to damp skin for maximum hydration — it needs water to work.

Ceramides

Barrier

Also listed as: Ceramide NP, AP, EOP

Lipids that make up ~50% of the skin's protective barrier. They lock moisture in and keep irritants out. Ceramide-rich moisturisers are ideal for dry, sensitive, or compromised skin barriers.

Dry skin Sensitive skin Moisturiser

Glycerin

Gentle

Also listed as: Glycerol

One of the most effective and well-studied humectants. Draws moisture into the skin and helps maintain the skin barrier. Found in almost every moisturiser for good reason.

All skin types Toner / Moisturiser

Squalane

Gentle

Also listed as: Hydrogenated Olive Oil

A lightweight, plant-derived oil that mimics the skin's natural sebum. Non-comedogenic and excellent for all skin types including oily skin — it balances oil production without clogging pores.

All skin types Non-comedogenic Face oil

⚗️ Exfoliating Actives

Niacinamide

Superstar

Also listed as: Vitamin B3, Nicotinamide

One of skincare's most versatile actives. Minimises pores, regulates oil, fades dark spots, strengthens the barrier, and reduces redness. Works for virtually every skin concern.

All skin types 5–10% concentration AM & PM
Pairs well with: Retinol, Hyaluronic Acid, AHAs, SPF.

AHAs

Exfoliant

Glycolic Acid, Lactic Acid, Mandelic Acid

Water-soluble acids that exfoliate the skin's surface, improving texture, brightness, and fading dark spots. Glycolic is the strongest; lactic and mandelic are gentler and better for sensitive skin.

Normal–oily–dry PM use Follow with SPF
Caution: Increases sun sensitivity — always wear SPF the next morning.

BHAs

Exfoliant

Salicylic Acid, Beta Hydroxy Acid

Oil-soluble acids that penetrate deep into pores to dissolve sebum and dead skin cells. Ideal for oily, acne-prone, and congested skin. Also has anti-inflammatory properties.

Oily / Acne-prone 0.5–2% concentration PM use

Centella Asiatica

Calming

Also listed as: Cica, Gotu Kola, TECA

A medicinal herb with powerful anti-inflammatory and wound-healing properties. Soothes redness, repairs the barrier, and is especially good for post-acne skin and sensitivity.

Sensitive skin Post-acne AM & PM

✨ Brighteners & Dark Spot Fighters

Vitamin C

Must-Have

L-Ascorbic Acid, Ascorbyl Glucoside, SAP

A potent antioxidant that brightens skin, fades hyperpigmentation, boosts collagen, and protects against UV damage. L-Ascorbic Acid is the most potent but least stable form.

AM use 10–20% concentration Pair with SPF
Storage: Keep in a cool, dark place. Discard if it turns orange.

Alpha Arbutin

Gentle

Also listed as: 4-Hydroxyphenyl-alpha-D-glucopyranoside

A stable, gentle ingredient that inhibits melanin production to fade dark spots and hyperpigmentation. Slower-acting than Vitamin C but much more tolerable for sensitive skin.

All skin types AM & PM 1–2% concentration

Kojic Acid

Use carefully

Derived from fungi during fermentation

Inhibits melanin production and brightens dark spots. Effective but can cause irritation at higher concentrations. Often found in serums and spot treatments.

Normal–oily skin 1% concentration PM use
Note: Patch-test first. Avoid if skin is irritated or compromised.

Azelaic Acid

Gentle

Naturally occurring in wheat, rye, barley

Multi-tasking ingredient that reduces redness, fades post-acne marks, fights acne-causing bacteria, and brightens skin. Safe during pregnancy (consult your doctor).

Sensitive / Acne-prone 10–20% AM & PM

⏳ Anti-Aging

Retinol

Powerhouse

Vitamin A, Retinoids, Retinal, Tretinoin

The gold standard for anti-aging. Accelerates cell turnover, boosts collagen, fades dark spots, and smooths wrinkles. Start low (0.025–0.05%) and build up frequency gradually.

PM use only Start 1×/week Always use SPF
Beginners: Start 1–2 nights per week, moisturise over it to minimise irritation.

Peptides

Gentle

Matrixyl, Argireline, Copper Peptides

Short chains of amino acids that signal the skin to produce more collagen and elastin. Gentle and effective for firming, plumping, and reducing the appearance of fine lines.

All skin types AM & PM Serum / Moisturiser

☀️ Sunscreen

Chemical SPF

Active

Avobenzone, Octinoxate, Tinosorb S/M

Absorbs UV rays and converts them to heat. Lightweight, invisible finish — ideal for daily use under makeup. Apply 15–20 minutes before sun exposure.

Normal–oily skin SPF 30–50+ Reapply every 2hr

Mineral SPF

Gentle

Zinc Oxide, Titanium Dioxide

Sits on top of the skin and physically reflects UV rays. Immediate protection, reef-safe, and gentle for sensitive or reactive skin. May leave a white cast on deeper skin tones.

Sensitive skin Pregnancy-safe SPF 30–50+

🔬 Ingredient Compatibility

Combination Compatible? Notes
Vitamin C + SPF ✓ Yes Powerful antioxidant + UV protection synergy. Apply Vitamin C first, let it absorb, then SPF.
Niacinamide + Retinol ✓ Yes Niacinamide helps buffer retinol irritation. Apply together in PM.
Niacinamide + Vitamin C ⚠ Caution Generally fine for most skin types. Layer separately or use different times if sensitive.
AHA + BHA ⚠ Caution Can be layered for enhanced exfoliation, but may irritate sensitive skin. Start slowly.
Retinol + AHA/BHA ✗ Avoid Both are exfoliating — together they cause irritation and barrier damage. Use on alternate nights.
Retinol + Vitamin C ✗ Avoid Different pH requirements reduce efficacy. Use Vitamin C in AM, Retinol in PM.
Hyaluronic Acid + Everything ✓ Yes Works harmoniously with all actives. Always a safe addition to any routine.
Peptides + AHAs ⚠ Caution Acids can degrade peptides at low pH. Layer peptides after acids have fully absorbed.
Niacinamide + AHA/BHA ✓ Yes Niacinamide helps soothe post-exfoliation redness. Apply after acid has absorbed.

📋 Routine Order

1

Cleanser

Start with a gentle, pH-balanced cleanser to remove overnight products and prepare skin.

e.g. CeraVe Hydrating Cleanser, Cetaphil Gentle Cleanser

2

Toner (optional)

Balance pH and add a first layer of hydration. Skip if your cleanser doesn't disrupt your barrier.

e.g. Hydrating toner or essence

3

Vitamin C Serum

Your antioxidant protection. Apply on clean, slightly damp skin and let it absorb for 1–2 minutes.

e.g. L-Ascorbic Acid 10–15%, Ascorbyl Glucoside

4

Treatment Serum

Niacinamide, hyaluronic acid, or peptides. Thinner formulas go before thicker ones.

5

Eye Cream

Apply with your ring finger — the lightest touch — to avoid tugging delicate skin.

6

Moisturiser

Lock in all the layers. Choose your formula based on skin type: gel for oily, cream for dry.

e.g. CeraVe Moisturising Cream

7

Sunscreen SPF 30+

The most important step. Apply generously — about a teaspoon for face and neck. Reapply every 2 hours outdoors.

1

Double Cleanse

Oil cleanser first to remove SPF and makeup; then a water-based cleanser for a thorough cleanse.

2

Toner / Essence

Hydrating toner or essence to prep skin for treatment products.

3

Exfoliant (2–3× per week)

AHA or BHA — not every night. On non-exfoliant nights, use your treatment serum instead.

e.g. Glycolic Acid 5–7%, Salicylic Acid 1–2%

4

Treatment Serum

Retinol, Niacinamide, peptides, or targeted actives. Never use Retinol on the same night as AHAs/BHAs.

5

Eye Cream

If using a retinoid near the eyes, apply a plain moisturiser as a buffer first.

6

Moisturiser / Night Cream

Richer formulas are great for PM — your skin repairs itself overnight. Seal in all treatments.

7

Face Oil (optional)

Apply oil last — it seals everything below. A few drops of squalane for extra nourishment.

📖 Glossary

Humectant
Draws water from the environment or deeper skin layers to hydrate the surface. Examples: Hyaluronic acid, glycerin.
Emollient
Softens and smooths skin by filling gaps between cells. Examples: Squalane, shea butter, fatty acids.
Occlusive
Forms a barrier on skin to prevent moisture loss. Examples: Petrolatum, dimethicone, beeswax.
Non-comedogenic
Formulated not to clog pores. Important for oily or acne-prone skin types.
Antioxidant
Neutralises free radicals from pollution and UV exposure. Prevents premature aging. Examples: Vitamin C, E, resveratrol.
PAO
Period After Opening — the small jar symbol showing how many months a product is safe to use after opening.
pH
Skin's natural pH is ~5.5 (slightly acidic). Cleansers and actives that match this work best and cause least disruption.
Skin Barrier
The outermost layer of skin that protects against external irritants and retains moisture. Ceramides and fatty acids maintain it.
Hyperpigmentation
Darkening of skin patches caused by excess melanin — triggered by sun, acne, hormones, or injury.
Cell Turnover
The process by which old skin cells shed and new ones emerge. Slows with age; retinoids and AHAs speed it up.
